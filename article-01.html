No Universal Baseline

A note on individuality, measurement, and learning to read your own physiological patterns.

In medical literature, population averages provide convenient benchmarks. Yet for any single individual, an abstract average rarely functions as a true reference point. The female body operates within a dynamic spectrum of monthly fluctuations, environmental responses, and physiological shifts.

When physiological signals—such as heart rate variability, nocturnal temperature, and sleep architecture—are interpreted solely against generic standards, nuanced individual shifts are easily misread as anomalies or ignored altogether.

Phasica begins from a different premise: your body is its own baseline. By modeling your individual signals over time, observation turns into self-knowledge, grounded in precise, quiet measurement rather than arbitrary norms.
